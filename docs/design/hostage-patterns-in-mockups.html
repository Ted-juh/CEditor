1 · A MIDI file on the grid

Drop a drum loop's file on the drum machine. The whole file is drawn small; choose the bars that become the pattern, up to its 64 steps. Each note goes to the drum on that note, or offers a new lane. The feel can be kept: each hit snaps to its step and keeps how early or late it was, as the step's nudge. A whole song's file is offered as a MIDI backing file instead, play-only.

2 · The groove from your playing

Nothing to arm: HoSTage keeps the last two minutes of everything played. Play along with the click on the pads, then "Take the groove from what I just played". It finds where each sixteenth sat (how early or late) and how hard, averaged over the bars, and keeps it as a groove of your own, to put on any pattern.

3 · Beatbox it in

First, teach it your voice: each sound four times. Then beatbox along with the click: each sound becomes a hit on its drum, on the grid, with how sure HoSTage is. Your voice is not kept, only the hits. Mistakes are fixed on the grid like any hit.

Teach it your sounds

"Boom"Kick · 4 of 4

"Tss"Closed hat · 4 of 4
+ soundan open hat, a clap: say it four timesTeach
Taught once a voice and kept with the microphone: anyone's "boom" differs, so HoSTage learns yours rather than guessing.
On speakers the mic hears the click too. The count-in listens to what leaks in and hears only what is louder; headphones are better.

4 · On the CTRL49

Taking the groove and beatboxing are things you do at the keyboard, so each has a page, reached from KIT and PATTERN. A MIDI file is on the computer, so dropping one stays in the dock. Which knob and pad does what is settled when the pages are built.
