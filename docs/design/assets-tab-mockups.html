The Assets tab

1,073px, a media library picked from two dropdowns, and a filmstrip frame count that can be wrong in a way nothing in the application checks.

Built on 2026-09-10. These are still drawings, not screenshots, and the shipped tab differs from them in two places: frame boundaries are the gaps between separate frame boxes rather than drawn lines, and the offered repairs are computed divisors (150, 100, 90 for a 900px strip) rather than the illustrative numbers below. The asset shapes and the slicing arithmetic were read out of CustomAssetsEditor.svelte and InteractivePartRenderer.svelte and are unchanged.
Plate 1

The frame count can be wrong, and nothing says so

This is the finding the tab is worth building for. InteractivePartRenderer slices a filmstrip with proportional CSS background positioning — background-size: 100% (frameCount × 100)% — so if the strip's height does not divide evenly by the frame count the frames drift, and at the extremes one shows a sliver of its neighbour. A 128-frame strip with 127 typed in is broken in a way that looks like a rendering bug.

The check is arithmetic the app already has everything for. The asset stores its source; imageDimensions() in the editor already measures the natural size on import. height % frameCount === 0 is the whole test, and it has never been run.

How the three open questions were decided

  • How much of Assets? Images, filmstrips and packagePolicy. The fonts and thumbnails maps were left out: nothing reads them, nothing writes them and no export path carries them.
  • The frame readout versus the no-sliders rule. No scrub track. Step buttons, a typed frame number, and clicking a frame on the strip — three ways in, none of them a slider.
  • What to do when the count does not divide. Warn and repair. The bar names the arithmetic and offers the nearest counts that do divide, each labelled with the frame size it produces; one click applies it.
