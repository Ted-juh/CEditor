1 · Practice: the notes fall onto your keyboard

A MIDI file opened in the Practice tab. Your hand's notes fall in blue onto the keys they belong to, each as long as the note; the other hand falls in grey and is played by HoSTage through your own rig. Keys light green when right and red when wrong, with how early or late. Slower tempo, wait mode, which hand, and a loop with count-in sit in the bar above.

2 · Opening a song: who plays what

Every track of the file is listed with its range and a picture of where its notes are. Each is played by you (as the left or the right hand), by the rig, or not at all. What the rig plays goes in as if played on your keyboard, through your splits and layers, unless a track is sent to one part. A file with one track is split into hands at a key. All of this is kept for the song.

When the loop comes round or the song ends: how many notes were right, how many on time, a dot per note across the bars (early above, late below, wrong in red), the bars that went worst with a button to loop just those, and the runs so far. When two runs in a row are over 90 %, it offers the next tempo. Kept per song on this computer; nothing is sent anywhere.

4 · PRACTICE on the CTRL49

Both hands stay on the keys: the controller starts, stops, loops and slows the song, and shows the next notes for wait mode. The same rules as every page: knobs set values, pads switch options, pad 8 goes back.

Knobs

  • 1 Tempo, 2 and 3 the loop's first and last bar, 4 the count-in, 5 the rig's level against your hand.

Pads

  • Play, restart (from the loop's start, with the count-in), wait, loop.
  • Left, right, both: which hand you play.
  • Back to the page before.

The card

  • The next notes for your hand, the one due first lit; where you are in the song and the loop; the last run's result.
