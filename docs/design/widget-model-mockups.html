A widget model, drawn

Six mockups of the proposal in widget-model-creator-plan.md: what one dial costs to author today, what a widget inspector would hold instead, and the on-canvas handles that replace the number fields.

Nothing here is built. These are drawings, not screenshots. Every colour and control metric is taken from the real surface so the pictures sit next to the application honestly, but no code in this repository produces any of these screens.

Plate 3  ·  revised 2026-09-10

The one handle worth building

The first draft of this plate drew draggable arc ends, draggable slider track endpoints and a draggable grab-area outline. Those are withdrawn as too fiddly. An angle you set by dragging a 7px dot on a 168px circle is a worse control than a number field — it cannot be typed, nudged or copied. A handle has to beat the field it replaces, and for a value with an exact number in it the field usually wins.

What survives is the case where there is no good field to begin with. A count tells you nothing until you stop typing and look, so you type, look, retype. Dragging it shows you the answer while you choose it.

Plate 5

Unpack

One command, one direction. The widget node is deleted, its parts, channel, behavior and zone are released into the flat lists, and nothing rebuilds over the top afterwards. This is Figma's "detach instance" and Illustrator's "expand", and it reuses the scissors control already in the inspector header for generated layers.

Plate 6  ·  revised 2026-09-10

Other widget types, same shape

This plate was drawn to test a rule — a widget holds at most eight controls — that has since been withdrawn. The cap treated the number of properties as the problem. It is not: a font legitimately has forty settings, and hiding thirty-two behind a More button makes them harder to find, not easier to set. The problem is the space they occupy, which is the space plan's subject.

The three inspectors below still stand as drawings of what a widget panel looks like. They are no longer a budget being met.

What these drawings assume

Worth stating, because a mockup can quietly assume a decision that has not been made:

  • Widgets do not nest. There is no "Filter Section" widget containing three dials. That is deliberately out of scope for the first five phases; the package and library system is the composition story instead.
  • A widget owns its primitives exclusively. The child rows in Plate 4 are readable and selectable, but not independently editable while the widget exists. Editing them means Unpack.
  • The runtime does not change. Everything drawn here compiles down to the Parts, ValueChannels, Behaviors and HitZones the player renders today. The player never sees a widget.
  • Advanced mode survives. The More ▸ footer opens the existing editors unchanged. Nothing in the current creator is removed.
  • Nothing is capped. A widget panel holds what the widget needs. Its wide groups — effects, typography, fills — route to the display panel like every other editor's, per the space plan.
