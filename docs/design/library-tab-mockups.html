The Library tab

A library you cannot place from, a palette that shows six, and a picture of each saved component that is missing both ends of its layer stack.

Built on 2026-09-10. The space argument for this candidate was measured first and rejected — see below. What is built rests on the three findings, each checked against the shipped code before this page was written.
Plate 1

Plate 2

Fourteen of twenty-two, missing from both ends

createCustomComponentThumbnail reduces a component to at most 18 coloured rectangles; the card then draws the first 14 of those. Two caps, in two files, and neither says anything when it bites. The envelope sorts by z-index ascending and keeps the last 18 — so what it discards is the bottom of the stack, the background before anything else.

The component · 22 parts

Bottom of the stack at the bottom.

The envelope · keeps 18

.slice(-18) — the background goes first.

The card · draws 14

.slice(0, 14) — and now the top four as well.

The entry carries envelope.component, the whole control. The tab draws that, with the renderer that draws it on the canvas.

Plate 3

Both ways to use a package, named for what they do. Placing a copy has never been available from this data; replacing has been the only option, and has never said whose sections it was about to take.

Decided while building

  • This tab follows the selection, unlike the four editing tabs which stay on the control they were opened with. Replace must overwrite what you are pointing at, and a stale target here would destroy the wrong component.
  • Nothing was removed. Save, import, export, metadata and validation stay in the properties panel — the parts of this that really are the modal task the candidate list suspected. The Insert panel is untouched too.
  • The two thumbnail caps stay. The tab draws the real thing and reports the loss; changing what a saved envelope contains is a data-format change, not a dock tab's to make.
