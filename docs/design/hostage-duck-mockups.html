1 · The kick ducks the bass, in the dock

A DUCK block, HoSTage's own, sits before the bass part's Out: on every kick the bass's level dips and comes back, so kick and bass stop fighting. No audio sidechain: HoSTage knows when every kick plays, the pattern's and the ones played on the pads. The picture is one bar: the kicks above, the bass's level under them.

Checks
Pattern kicks dip the bass on their step; kicks played on the pads dip it as they are played.
The kick is a Samples pad: no delay. A hardware kick would make the dip wait for its sound (its measured lateness), not for its MIDI.
"An input (later)": a real kick drum's microphone needs the audio sidechain the first round left for later (effects 6). This covers every kick HoSTage plays or receives.

2 · DUCK on the CTRL49

The bar at the keyboard's size, the kicks and the bass's level under them, as the duck is set. Knob 1 chooses what ducks it; then depth, attack, hold and release; the last two the second part it ducks and how much.

What it is, and is not

  • HoSTage's own level, moved on every hit, like the limiter and the gate: a utility, no sound of its own (first round, decision 3).
  • Not a compressor listening to audio: it reacts to the hits HoSTage plays or receives, so it is exact and costs nothing.
  • An acoustic kick drum on a microphone needs the audio sidechain, still later (first round, effects 6).
