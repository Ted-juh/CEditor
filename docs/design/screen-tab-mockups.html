The Screen tab

3,814px across two editors, a page order that cannot be reordered, and a zone that can slide off the screen with nothing anywhere saying so.

Built on 2026-09-10. These are still drawings, and the shipped tab differs from them in one place: an out-of-range region is drawn where the renderer will really paint it — a clamped zone on the cell it collapses to, one that paints nowhere as a tab on the edge it fell off — rather than as a box at its literal numbers, which put an amber rectangle over the settings column. The heights were measured for real, both editors mounted in Chromium with getBoundingClientRect(), and the placement arithmetic is quoted out of utils/lcdZones.js.
Plate 1

A zone can fall off the screen, and nothing says so

This is the finding the tab is worth building for. composeLayout drops a zone whose row is past the last row, and clamps one whose columns are past the last column — which is worse, because a clamped zone still paints. Nine cells at columns 20–28 on a 16-column screen become one cell hard against the right edge.

The trigger is two clicks. Rows and Cols are number wells at the top of the same panel; taking a 4×20 screen down to 2×16 sends half the layout somewhere you cannot see, and the table still shows the numbers you typed.

How the three open questions were decided

  • One tab, two modes. The model speaks in rects over a grid and converts at the edges, so layouts, page rules and the check are written once. The stage is the one place the two are not flattened — and for a good reason: PixelDisplayRenderer already drags and resizes its elements, with snapping and grouped moves, while LcdDisplayRenderer has no drag code at all. The tab uses the shipped one and adds the missing one.
  • The spatial half only. Layouts, Pages and Zones/Elements. Screen size, Colour, Lighting and Motion stay in the panel: a dock that took them all would be a second properties panel.
  • Drag the box, drag its ends, snapped to cells, with the numbers beside it — the Typography settlement, where handles are for values that are a shape and the numbers stay.
