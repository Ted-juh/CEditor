Making room, not cutting properties

Three drawings for property-panel-space-plan.md: how tall the panel actually is, what the same editor looks like in the dock instead, and the one refactor that has to happen first.

Nothing here is built. Drawings, not screenshots. The heights in Plate 1 are computed from the source and the stylesheet, so they are real numbers; the layouts are proposals.

Plate 2

Portrait panel, landscape dock

The argument is not that the dock is bigger. It is that it is the right shape, and that it can show you the result while you edit. The panel is 600px wide and four columns; the dock sits in the centre column, so on a 1920px display it is about 1,264px wide with a ceiling around 820px. Text effects are 58 controls that each change how a glyph looks — the specimen is the point of the editor, and there is nowhere to put one in a 600px strip.

One target store, not four

Plate 3

After this, adding a dock tab is registering a row rather than writing a fourth store. impliedDockTab reads the registry instead of a hardcoded if-chain.

The selection line is a bug fix, not a nicety. In BackgroundEditor.svelte the field route writes through updateSelectedProperty when more than one control is selected, while the swatch route builds a target carrying a single controlId. Read from the source that means: with three controls selected, typing a hex paints three and picking the same colour through the dock paints one. Worth confirming in the running app before building on it — but if it holds, every group moved to the dock inherits it.
