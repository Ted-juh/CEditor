1 · Keys and velocity, in one picture

Every part is a lane across the keyboard, as on the CTRL49's LAYERS page; inside its lane, height is velocity, so a part that plays only hard notes fills only the top of its keys. A layer group is a bracket joining its lanes; a crossfade is a soft edge. Play a note and a line drops through every lane: a dot where it sounds, and how loud. Drag an edge to change keys or velocity, or set it by playing.

2 · Layer two parts

Select two lanes and choose how they share the keys. Four ways, each drawn and said in one line, the ones a layer group already offers. The chosen one opens in words: who plays when, and how the change-over sounds. A group by a controller shows where the controller is now and each part's share at that point.

Layer L3 · round robin

Snare A · Snare B

Each new note goes to the next part in turn, so two identical hits never sound the same. Least busy instead sends it to whichever has the fewest notes sounding.
OrderA, B, A, B…Last notewent to Snare B
Round robin and least busy pick one part per note; the other two ways weigh every member.

3 · LAYERS on the CTRL49, grown

Left, today's page: a lane a part over the keys, velocity as text. Right, the same page with velocity inside each lane and the groups bracketed, as in the tab.
