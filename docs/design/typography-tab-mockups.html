The Typography tab

1,317px of properties, thirteen text-flow modes with no picture of any of them, and a section where roughly seven fields in ten do nothing at the moment you are looking at them.

Nothing here is built. Drawings, not screenshots. Field names, defaults and the per-mode parameter map are real — read out of sectionDefaults.js and canvasControlTextLayout.js.
No sliders, same as the Effects tab: NumberCell for numbers, chips for choices, and pickers where the choice is visual — the family list, the weight ramp and the mode grid.

Plate 3

The three modes with no numbers worth typing

Worth naming, because this is where the no-sliders rule and the no-fiddly-handles rule collide with the data. Bezier stores eight percentages. Polyline and freehand store arrays of points — and freehand's name says drawing. Nobody authors a curve by typing eight numbers, and there is no number-based way to author a point array at all.

The proposal below is curve presets plus the numbers underneath: every control stays typeable and no drag handles are added. The cost is that freehand cannot really be authored here, only adjusted — which is the open question.

Open

  • One target store or two? effectsTarget is a control id plus a domain and Typography is the same shape. A second near-identical store is the duplication the space plan complains about in colorTarget / gradientTarget; merging them into one editorTarget keyed by kind is tidier and touches shipped code.
  • Bezier, polyline and freehand. Presets plus numbers keeps both rules. Letting these three modes have draggable points on the specimen breaks the no-handles rule but is the only way they are genuinely authorable.
  • Does Multiline belong here? Wrapping, line height and overflow are layout rather than type, but they are in the same panel section today and the specimen shows the result.
