1. Drop it where it goes

A sound, an instrument or an effect is dragged from the browser. While it is held, every place that takes it is outlined; the one under the pointer is filled and says what will happen. The keyboard shows every part's band while something is held, as Range shows it now. Here a bass from Sounds is held over C3: it will become a new part, and because the library knows it is a bass, it takes the keys below.

1On a key: a new part split there (a bass takes the keys below, anything else the keys above; ⇄ flips it) 2On a part's band: a new part layered with it 3On a part's row: loads into that part, replacing what is there (undoable) 4Between rows or below them: a new part there 5An effect: onto the Chain, a row or a mixer strip

2. Work on parts, not one control at a time

Ctrl-click or Shift-click selects several parts; a bar above them changes what they share. Parts move by their grip. An effect or a MIDI module is dragged from the Chain onto another part's row to move it there, Ctrl to copy. Each row says what it costs, and offers to even its level when its sound is far louder or softer than the rest.

1Several parts: channel, transpose, output, an effect, duplicate, remove 2Grip: drag to reorder (the up and down buttons go) 3Duplicate a part, with its chain and zone 4Drag between parts: move, Ctrl copies 5What it costs, from its own worker 6Even level, offered, never automatic

3. Why is this not sounding?

Play a key: each row says whether the note reached it, and if not, why, in plain words. The dock follows what you play and shows the part that sounded (the top one when several did); the pin stops it following. Move a knob on the controller and the screen shows what it controls. Nothing here changes the rig; each reason is a link to the setting that caused it.

1Follow the note: the key held, its velocity and channel 2Each row: sounding, or the reason it stayed silent 3The dock follows what you play; pin to stop 4Touch to find: a knob moved names what it controls
